# Conventions and data

What exactly the numbers mean, where they come from, and why each choice was made.

## Output quantity

`position()` / `positionTT()` return the **apparent geocentric ecliptic longitude of date**
(tropical zodiac, true equinox and true ecliptic of date), in degrees in [0, 360), and its rate in
degrees per day. This is the quantity astrology software calls "the planet's position" and the
default output of the Swiss Ephemeris (`swe_calc_ut` without extra flags).

Steps from the JPL ephemeris to the tabulated value (computed offline with Skyfield 1.55):

1. Observer at the geocentre; light time iterated (planet at the retarded epoch).
2. Gravitational light deflection by the **Sun only**. Skyfield adds Jupiter and Saturn by
   default; JPL Horizons also deflects by the Sun only (its output headers state
   `Rel. light bend : Sun`). The choice was made by comparison with Swiss Ephemeris output: at
   the closest Uranus–Jupiter encounters 1899–2101 (56″ and 76″) the residual against the Swiss
   Ephemeris scatters by 0.14–0.20 mas with the Sun only and by 0.67–0.98 mas with Jupiter and
   Saturn (`benchmark/studies/deflection_study.py`).
3. For rays passing **behind the solar disc** the point-mass formula diverges (an unphysical 1/b
   spike). The tables use the mass inside the cylinder of impact parameter b for an n = 3
   polytrope (Eddington's solar model; Schneider, Ehlers & Falco 1992). This model, too, was
   chosen by comparison with Swiss Ephemeris output: at the deepest conjunctions (elongation
   0.001°–0.05°) the RMS residual against the Swiss Ephemeris is 0.03–0.08″ with the polytrope,
   0.36–14.6″ with the point-mass formula and 0.38–1.57″ without deflection
   (`benchmark/studies/deflection_study.py`). The body is hidden by the Sun there, so this only
   matters for continuity.
4. Relativistic annual aberration.
5. GCRS to the true equator and equinox of date: frame bias + IAU 2006 precession + **IAU 2000A
   nutation** (full series; IERS Conventions 2010, chapter 5), then rotation about x by the true
   obliquity (IAU 2006 mean obliquity + IAU 2000A Δε) into the true ecliptic of date.
6. Mars to Pluto are **system barycentres**, as in DE440 and as in the Swiss Ephemeris default.

**True lunar node:** the osculating node of h = r × v of the geocentric Moon from DE440s at the
light-time-retarded epoch, on the mean ecliptic of date (IAU 2006) plus Δψ, i.e. the true
ecliptic of date. (Nutation moves the equator, not the ecliptic, so the true ecliptic of date is
the mean ecliptic of date with the equinox shifted by Δψ; IERS Conventions 2010, eqs. 5.39–5.40.)
The variant was chosen by comparison with Swiss Ephemeris output
(`benchmark/studies/true_node_study.py`, 3,000 epochs): of the physically defined alternatives,
the one with the smallest RMS residual against `SE_TRUE_NODE` was taken; nothing was fitted. RMS /
max residual: retarded 0.0084″ / 0.18″ (mean +0.0001″), geometric instead of retarded
0.0095″ / 0.17″ (mean −0.0026″); RMS only: without frame bias 0.17″, mean equinox only 12.2″,
J2000 ecliptic 2891″.

**Chiron:** barycentric ICRF state vectors from JPL Horizons, orbit solution JPL#171 (observations
1895–2026), 4-day grid, cubic Hermite interpolation (estimated error 2·10⁻⁶″), then the same
reduction as the planets. The raw Horizons responses are committed under
`build/tables/sources/horizons/`, because Horizons always serves its current solution, which can
change.

## Time scales

- Tables are in **TT**. `positionTT(jdTT)` evaluates them directly; `position(jdUT1)` first adds
  ΔT = TT − UT1.
- `utcToJd()` from 1972: TT = UTC + (TAI − UTC) + 32.184 s with the IERS leap-second table;
  UT1 = TT − ΔT. Before 1972 the civil reading is treated as UT1 (the international UTC system
  with leap seconds starts on 1972-01-01). `23:59:60.x` is accepted on leap-second days only.
- **ΔT** (`deltaTTable.js`, generated by `build/delta-t/build_delta_t.py`):
  - 1898.75–1950.0: Morrison, Stephenson, Hohenkerk & Zawilski 2021 (spline table S15.2020);
  - 1950.0–1955.5: cubic Hermite blend between the two neighbouring sources (value and slope of
    Morrison et al. at 1950.0 and of the USNO values at 1955.5);
  - 1955.5–1962.0: USNO historic ΔT (atomic time);
  - 1962 to the last observation: IERS EOP 20 C04;
  - then IERS Bulletin A predictions to `IERS_LAST_JD` (2027-09-25);
  - after that a cubic Hermite curve from the last prediction (its value and the mean slope of
    its last year) towards the Stephenson–Morrison–Hohenkerk 2016 parabola, which it meets in
    2800 (the construction used by Skyfield 1.55).

  Table step 1/12 year, 4-point Lagrange interpolation.
- **Future UTC**: TAI − UTC stays at its last value (37 s). No further leap second has been
  announced, and the CGPM decided in 2022 (Resolution 4) to let |UT1 − UTC| exceed 0.9 s by 2035
  at the latest. A future UTC instant therefore maps to TT = UTC + 69.184 s; positions do not
  depend on the ΔT prediction, houses do. With the current table UT1 − UTC reaches about −1.2 s
  in 2035, −5 s in 2050 and −35 s in 2100.

## Houses

- Sidereal time: IAU 2006 GMST from the Earth rotation angle (IERS Conventions 2010, eq. 5.32 and
  5.15, as ERFA `gmst06`) plus the equation of the equinoxes with the IAU 2000 complementary
  terms (USNO Circular 179, section 2.6.2). Δψ and the true obliquity come from tables of the same
  IAU 2000A series. ARMC = GAST + geographic longitude (east positive).
- Placidus: trisection of the diurnal and nocturnal semi-arcs, solved to below 10⁻⁶″. Undefined
  inside the polar circles (|latitude| ≥ 90° − ε): `cusps: null, error: 'PLACIDUS_POLAR'`, with
  Ascendant and MC still given. The MC stays on the upper meridian; where the ecliptic crosses the
  horizon "the wrong way", the Ascendant follows the MC within 180°.
- Whole sign: cusp 1 at 0° of the Ascendant's sign, one sign per house, also in polar regions.

## Tables

Chebyshev records interpolated at the n Chebyshev–Lobatto points (record ends shared with the
neighbour; jump at boundaries < 10⁻⁸″) and verified at 24 evenly spaced points per record
including both ends. All tables cover JD TT 2414655.5 to 2488447.5 (1899-01-01 to 2101-01-14,
0h TT); in UTC that is from 1899-01-01 00:00:04 to 2101-01-13 23:58:50 (exact bounds in
`info().coverage`).

Next to a **superior solar conjunction** the light deflection (up to 1.75″ at the solar limb)
changes within hours, and a single 16-day polynomial misses it by up to about 7″ (Neptune 6.89″;
`maxArcsecBeforeRefinement` in the manifest's `sources.fitReport`). Since data format 2 every
record that passes within 3° of the Sun, or exceeds 0.5 mas at its check points, is checked at
401 points (≈ 1 hour apart) and, where needed, replaced by halved sub-records (`<name>.refined.bin`)
until each meets the 0.5 mas target at points at least as dense.

The fit error is verified at these dense check points (24 per record, 401 per record next to a
superior conjunction, and at least as dense for its sub-records). It is about 0.5 mas: the largest
error found there is 0.5002 mas (Mercury), marginally above the 0.5 mas target, because a
sub-record only has to meet the target at its own check points and the later dense check of the
whole record can find a slightly larger value. The table lists the maximum for
every table as recorded in `data/manifest.json` (`maxFitErrorArcsec`). These are the largest
errors found at the check points, not mathematically proven bounds between them.

Total 8,153,312 bytes (7.78 MiB). The binary format is documented at the top of
[`chebyshev.js`](../chebyshev.js): little-endian Float64 coefficients, value = Σ cₖ Tₖ(x),
derivative = (2/L) Σ cₖ Tₖ′(x), sub-records as `[start, end, c₀ … cₙ₋₁]`.

**Manifest provenance.** `data/manifest.json` contains
`"createdBy": "tools/ephemeris-build/build.py 1.1.0"`. That is the builder's path in Zodiacally's
internal repository, where the October 2026 build was made; in this repository the builder is
`build/tables/build.py`. The string is part of the manifest and therefore of its published
SHA-256 (`dc456976…19c35`), so it is kept unchanged, and `build.py` still writes it so that a
rebuild stays byte-identical. The `description` in `test/fixtures/reference.json` names the same
internal builder path ("generated by tools/ephemeris-build/build.py") and is kept for the same
reason: `build.py` writes it unchanged, so that a rebuild reproduces the committed fixture byte
for byte.

## How the Swiss Ephemeris was used

In this repository the Swiss Ephemeris (`sweph`) is used only as an external reference, through
its public API, in `benchmark/` and in the study scripts in
[`benchmark/studies/`](../benchmark/studies/); during development it was also used, the same
way, in Zodiacally's internal comparison harness (see [accuracy.md](accuracy.md)). No Swiss
Ephemeris source code was read or used, no Swiss Ephemeris data files are shipped, and no
coefficient or continuous parameter was fitted to its output. The tables are computed only from
JPL data via Skyfield; the builder never imports `sweph` or anything from `benchmark/`.

Three discrete modelling choices between published conventions were, however, selected by
comparing residuals against Swiss Ephemeris output:

1. light deflection by the Sun only, rather than Sun, Jupiter and Saturn (step 2 above; JPL
   Horizons also uses Sun-only deflection);
2. the n = 3 polytrope enclosed-mass model for rays behind the solar disc (step 3);
3. the light-time-retarded osculating node for the true node.

The residuals quoted above for these choices and their alternatives are the outputs of the
published study scripts (run on 8 November 2026 against the same SE1 files as the benchmark;
outputs in [`benchmark/studies/results/`](../benchmark/studies/results/)). For these three
conventions the agreement with the Swiss Ephemeris is therefore partly by construction, which is
why the comparison with NASA/JPL Horizons is reported as well (see [accuracy.md](accuracy.md) and
the [benchmark](../benchmark/README.md)).
